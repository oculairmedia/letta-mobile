# Research provenance for SPEC §10

The cited work motivates asymmetric blinks, intentional gaze, eyelid expression and squash/stretch. It does not establish this mascot's optimal pixel amplitudes, pupil frequencies, deformer weights or recognition at 44 dp. Those values are authored design decisions; geometry checks and visual proofs assess this asset pack separately from runtime and human recognition tests.

| Source | Evidence that transfers | Attribution limit / correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Onchi, Saakes and Lee, ISASE 2020, §§3–6](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/isase/ISASE2020/0/ISASE2020_1_45/_pdf/-char/en) | A study of 31 design students found that eyelid positions and simulated gloss changed emotional interpretations of a single-eye avatar. | The stimulus used iris diameter `0.7d`, pupil `0.25d`, gloss `0.2d`, where `d` means **the entire eye diameter**. These were fixed design choices, not optimized ratios. Its gloss was therefore `0.8 × pupil diameter`; a smaller catchlight here is an independent choice. The stimuli were static; pupil animation was explicitly untested. The paper does not validate a sine pupil, frequency table or geometric glyph sizing. |
| [Trutoiu et al., Disney Research 2011, Fig.3 and Experiment 2](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Modeling-and-Animating-Eye-Blinks-Paper.pdf) | Fast closure followed by slower opening; fully closing animated lids received higher naturalness ratings. Experiment 2 compared 7/9/11/13 frames at **30 fps**. The 7- and 9-frame conditions (about 233/300 ms) performed best, with no significant difference between them. | The orb's `4/1/10` frames at 60 fps is an authored approximation, not a measured paper prescription. Neither the automatic wait range nor double-blink probability is established by this paper. |
| [Lee and Badler et al., Eyes Alive 2002, author-uploaded paper, Table 1 and §5](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2534686_Eyes_Alive) | Recorded gaze favored cardinal directions. Table 1 gives right/up/left/down as 15.54/17.69/16.80/20.38%; diagonal bins range 6.46–7.89%. Their synthesis uses `duration_ms = 25 + 2.4 × rotation_degrees`. | The direction distribution comes from a limited conversational recording. Orb pixel offsets, hop/hold times and a normalized head-turn threshold of `0.4` are adaptations, not reported measurements. Pixel travel cannot replace rotation degrees in the equation. These results do not establish continuous sine deformation or cosmetic tremor frequencies. |
| [Pan et al., Disney Research / IROS 2020, Eq.2 and §IV-D](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/root.pdf) | Linear habituation and restoration; eye movements lead slower head movements. Their robot shifts gaze among a person's eyes and nose at random intervals of 0.1–0.5 s. | Habituation rates are customizable. The brief's decay/restoration rates, cursor threshold, 350 ms lag, spring coefficients and `1.15×` parallax are authored values. The robot's gaze implementation does not validate sine-shaped pupils. |
| [Garcia et al., APGV 2008, §§4.1–4.3](https://gv2.scss.tcd.ie/cosulliv/Pubs/APGV08Garcia.pdf) | Squash/stretch improved trajectory prediction in the studied tasks. Participants preferred the stylized game **63% of the time**; attention results were inconclusive. | “Almost twice as often” describes preference frequency, not a doubled appeal score. No particular lattice, weight, deformation percentage or mobile-size recognition threshold follows from the experiment. |
| [Apple, WWDC23: Design considerations for vision and motion](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10078/) | In spatial-computing guidance, Apple cautions about sustained oscillations, particularly around 0.2 Hz, and recommends reducing their prominence and providing an oscillation-free option. | This is a comfort caution, not a recommendation for a 4600 ms breath. It does not prove either the current or proposed mobile-orb period comfortable. Cite this session rather than attributing the numeric warning solely to the HIG Motion page. |

## Deformation terminology

`scaleY = 1/scaleX` preserves **2D area**, not 3D volume. For example, `scaleY=0.90` needs `scaleX=1.111111…` for exact area preservation; `0.90 × 1.09 = 0.981` loses 1.9%. Weighted nonuniform deformation needs its own area and containment check. Lattice coordinates and weights are authored, not research measurements.
